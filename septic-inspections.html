Why It Matters

A $10,000 Problem Hides Underground

A septic system is the most expensive component of a home that buyers never look at. A failing drain field or damaged tank can cost five figures to replace — and a standard home inspection barely touches the septic system. Before you close on a home with septic in North Georgia, have someone who actually installs and repairs these systems put eyes on it.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a regular home inspection cover the septic system?

Usually only briefly. Most home inspectors aren't septic contractors, so the tank, lines and drain field get little attention. A septic inspection from a company that installs and repairs these systems tells you what condition the system is really in.

What is a perc test?

A percolation (perc) test measures how quickly your soil absorbs water. It determines whether a lot can support a septic system and what type of system it needs. If you're building on raw land, it comes before anything else.

What happens if the inspection finds a problem?

We explain what we found in plain English, how serious it is, and what it would cost to repair or replace. That gives you real numbers to take to the closing table, or peace of mind if the system checks out.

When should I schedule a pre-purchase septic inspection?

As early in your due-diligence period as possible, so there's time to get repair estimates or negotiate before closing.

Where do you do septic inspections?
